Payment Risk Scoring
An interactive fraud detection engine built on XGBoost, trained on 284,807 real credit card transactions. Adjust the thresholds below to explore the tradeoff between fraud capture and customer friction.
About this Project
Overview, methodology & results

Problem & Motivation

Credit card fraud costs the global financial industry over $30 billion annually. Traditional rule-based systems generate excessive false positives, frustrating legitimate customers. This project builds an end-to-end ML pipeline that assigns a continuous risk score to each transaction, mirroring how production systems at companies like Revolut, Monzo and Visa operate. The dataset contains 284,807 transactions with only 0.17% fraud cases, a realistic and challenging class imbalance scenario.

Dataset

Source: Kaggle / ULB Machine Learning Group.

Features V1–V28 are PCA-transformed (anonymised for confidentiality). Amount is the transaction value in EUR; Time is seconds elapsed since the first transaction. No missing values. 492 fraud cases across two days.

Class Imbalance: SMOTE

Standard accuracy is misleading when 99.83% of transactions are legitimate. SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique) generates synthetic fraud samples during training only, applied carefully to prevent data leakage into the validation set.

Feature Engineering

Three engineered features boosted signal beyond the raw PCA components: Amount_log (log-transform to reduce skew), Hour (extracted from Time for within-day patterns), and Is_night (binary flag for the 22:00–06:00 window, which correlates strongly with fraud).

Evaluation Strategy

ROC-AUC alone is insufficient on imbalanced data. Primary metrics: PR-AUC (Precision-Recall AUC), Recall on fraud class (how much fraud did we catch?), and Precision on fraud class (false alarm rate for operations teams).

Estimated Business Impact

Applied to a hypothetical platform processing 1 million transactions/day at £50 average value: ~1,700 fraudulent transactions occur daily. XGBoost catches ~1,470 of them (87% recall), protecting an estimated ~£73,000/day in fraud value. At the default cutoff about 42% of flagged transactions are fraud, so the threshold controls below show how to trade a little recall for far fewer false alarms.

Key Learnings

Class imbalance is the core challenge. SMOTE and PR-AUC are essential: standard accuracy is a misleading metric on this data.
Threshold tuning is a business decision. The right cutoff depends on operational capacity and risk appetite, which is exactly what this dashboard lets you explore.
Feature engineering amplified signal. Time-of-day and log-amount meaningfully improved recall across all three models, even alongside 28 PCA components.
XGBoost gave the best balance on this data. It had the highest PR-AUC and F1 of the three models, the metrics that matter most on imbalanced fraud data, while Random Forest edged it slightly on ROC-AUC.
